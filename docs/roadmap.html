Five things we learned today

1

ARLO has been flying blind on recycling since April. The database key it used for recycle data was revoked on 2026-04-02. Every weekly update since then quietly fell back to the wiki. Nobody noticed because the wiki mostly agrees. The feed is now replaced.

2

Quest needs never made it into the database. The quest reader looked for a field the API does not have, so it found nothing, every time. The "After Rain Comes" line you see today came from wiki prose, not from the quest feed. Also fixed.

3

Your instinct about recycling was right, and it has a name. In a raid the action is called Salvage and it uses its own table, usually a smaller one. Power Bank gives 2 Battery and 2 Wires at home but only 2 Battery in the field. Both tables are public, and three independent sources agree on them for almost every item.

4

ARC Tracker is a vision pipeline, and we can borrow the shape without the code. A detector finds every slot, a learned model names the icon, a guided stash scroll builds a ledger, and it only scans when the stash is open and still. Their trick we get for free: when the tooltip names an item, remember what its icon looked like. ARLO already reads the tooltip.

5

Badges on every cell are the one risky piece on Wayland. Dozens of little windows will not do. We need one transparent click-through layer over the game, which the current toolkit cannot draw. A short experiment decides the approach before any badge code is written.

  2. 1 · Chips on the tooltip ready to try

    Built, reviewed twice, and waiting for one thing: you, with the game open.

  3. 1a · Roman numerals, done right ready to try

    Tiered weapons like Anvil II or Arpeggio IV are identified correctly even when the text reader drops a stroke. ARLO now knows which tiers exist for each weapon, and it counts the strokes in the picture of the numeral as a second opinion. A capture library lets you record real tooltips by hovering, so we measure the improvement instead of guessing.

    Built and reviewed. The stroke counter has only seen synthetic shapes, so your captures are the next step.

  4. 2 · Advice with a reason half done

    ARLO now has the list of every quest, expedition project, and workshop level that needs an item, including Scrappy, Gunsmith, the Research Station, and the Outpost. From that it will tell you "Keep 3 for Gunsmith IV, then sell" instead of just "Keep", and the popup gets its overhaul.

    The data is gathered and reviewed. The advice engine and the new popup come next, after the chips are confirmed working in game.

  5. 3 · Read the grid

    ARLO will find every slot in your open stash and recognize the icon in each one, without you hovering. Slots are found with classic image processing. Icons are recognized by a small learned model that compares each icon against a reference set, including crops learned from your own hovering. A simpler fingerprint approach is tried first as a baseline, and is expected to fail on lookalikes, which is what justifies the model.

    Not started. Needs phase 1.

  6. 4 · Badges on every cell

    Every recognized slot gets a badge: owned over needed, colored by the advice, hidden while you scroll. First a short experiment proves we can draw one transparent click-through layer over the game on Wayland, because that is the risky part.

    Not started. Needs phases 2 and 3.

  7. 5 · The stash ledger

    One slow scroll through your stash teaches ARLO what you own. The stash header count anchors the scroll so rows are not double counted. After that, ARLO keeps the count current from what it sees, and flags a category as unsure when the numbers stop adding up.

    Not started. Needs phase 3.

  8. 6 · Progress from the screen

    ARLO will read your workshop and project pages when you open them, so the advice counts down as you upgrade. Until then, a manual setting for your current levels does the same job.

    Not started. Needs phases 2 and 5, plus screenshots of your project pages at your resolution.

Technical notes, for when you want them

How ARLO works today. A scanner thread reads a fixed screen region for the word INVENTORY every half second. When it sees it, it reads a crop next to the cursor every 0.3 seconds to get the hovered item's name. The crop is masked to the tooltip's cream background, which is why finding the tooltip rectangle was cheap. One click-through popup draws the result in a corner. The database has six columns per item, and quest, project, and workshop needs lived only as wiki prose in one text field. On Wayland the capture keeps the latest full frame in memory, so a full-screen grab costs one crop. 91 tests pass.

What recognition each tool uses. ARLO uses Tesseract OCR for everything and no image library. ARC Tracker 2.3.2 uses a YOLO11 slot detector and a DINOv2 embedding gallery of 1,416 crops over 915 items for icons, a separate weapon tier classifier, glyph reading for stack counts, and rarity from the colored disc, with OCR only for tooltip titles and progress pages. OpenCV is in its bundle as plumbing: letterboxing, batch preparation, slot bounds, and finding the tooltip card. The decision for ARLO: OpenCV for geometry and gating, an embedding model for icon identity, OCR for text.

Data sources chosen. ARC Tracker's public JSON API for items (price, stack, rarity, recycle and salvage maps, stable icon URLs), quests (all nine Frozen Trail quests with structured needs), and projects (Expedition 1 to 5 with dates). MetaForge for workshop levels, the only source with Outpost 1 to 4. The wiki stays as fallback. Twelve recycle rows disagree between the APIs and the wiki, and Anvil and Arpeggio tiers look swapped between them, which wants an in-game check.

Roman numerals. Rarity was checked as a tier signal and rejected: Anvil I through IV are all Rare, because rarity belongs to the base weapon. The fix uses the catalog of 112 tiered names (some bases also exist untiered, such as Rascal) and a stroke count from the numeral's own pixels.
